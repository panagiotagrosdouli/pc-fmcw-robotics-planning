# Predictive Connectivity-Aware Motion Planning with a PC-FMCW-Informed Analytical Link Model

## Abstract

We study whether trajectory-conditioned future connectivity estimates can improve autonomous motion decisions relative to reactive connectivity scoring in a controlled PC-FMCW-informed simulation. Five planners share candidate generation, vehicle and road limits, static filtering, and predicted dynamic-target safety. P4 alone receives simulator future truth, and only for connectivity forecasting. A frozen development rule selected a 0.5 m planning buffer before 50 independent confirmatory seeds were opened. The confirmatory hard gate recorded zero collision, no-candidate, and modeled static-violation episodes. Relative to P1, P2 reduced mean modeled outage by 0.01740 (95% deterministic-bootstrap interval 0.01434–0.02029 in magnitude) and improved all five declared communication endpoints after Holm correction. P3 was worse than P2 on all five endpoints; P4 did not significantly differ from P2. A separately authorized directional-versus-distance-only study supports directional geometry as the operative mechanism, but includes one no-candidate episode and is not safety evidence. These results support predictive connectivity utility under the evaluated analytical model; they do not validate an optical channel or real-road safety.

## 1. Research question

Under a frozen PC-FMCW-informed analytical connectivity model, does predictive trajectory-conditioned scoring (P2) improve modeled communication outcomes relative to reactive scoring (P1) when the planners share the same motion and safety information?

## 2. Contributions and related-work position

The contribution is a controlled perception-to-action evaluation downstream of a PC-FMCW sensing/tracking concept, a causal P1–P2 comparison, and seed-level confirmatory inference with gated mechanism analysis. We do not claim novelty for generic communication-aware planning, optical trajectory optimization, or ISAC-aware motion planning.

## 3. System and model definition

The simulator supplies target motion; noisy observations feed a common endpoint-anchored damped lateral/constant-velocity forecast. Candidate ego trajectories are generated by one bounded dynamics lattice. The analytical link maps relative distance and direction to modeled SNR, BER, outage, and goodput. Upstream waveform/context parameters are kept separate from downstream geometry-to-link assumptions. The reported 193.4 THz carrier is retained as an upstream simulation parameter and is not reconciled with the laser-headlamp/blue-light interpretation.

## 4. Methods and information boundaries

- P0 ignores connectivity in its objective.
- P1 repeats current target state for myopic connectivity scoring.
- P2 scores candidate-dependent future connectivity using the common forecast.
- P3 propagates forecast uncertainty by Monte Carlo risk scoring.
- P4 uses future simulator target truth only in connectivity scoring.

All planners receive the same common forecast for dynamic safety. P4 cannot use truth to bypass a safety rejection. Candidate generation, vehicle limits, road bounds, static filtering, physical target clearance, stopping checks, and fallback behavior are shared.

## 5. Experimental protocol

V3–V5 development failures and the V6 confirmatory failure are preserved. V7 changed one predeclared estimator detail before untouched V7 seeds were inspected. Development used seeds 18000–18019 over seven safety margins. The minimum-passing rule selected 0.5 m. Confirmatory seeds 19000–19049 were opened only after selection; geometry seeds 20000–20019 were opened only after the confirmatory hard gate passed.

## 6. Baselines and metrics

The causal primary comparison is P2–P1. P3–P2 evaluates uncertainty/risk propagation, and P4–P2 measures the connectivity-only oracle gap. Declared communication endpoints are mean outage probability, mean SNR, minimum SNR, modeled BER, and modeled goodput. Collision, no-candidate, static violation, and buffer relaxation are reported separately.

## 7. Statistical analysis

The independent unit is the seed. Scenario rows are paired within each seed and averaged to one seed-level effect. Deterministic bootstrap intervals use 100,000 resamples. Two-sided paired Wilcoxon tests are Holm-adjusted across the five endpoints within each predeclared comparison family. Candidate evaluations and timesteps are diagnostics, not independent samples.

## 8. Results

The 1,250-row confirmatory artifact contains 50 seeds × five scenarios × five planners, with no duplicate paired unit. The hard gate passed with zero collision episodes, zero no-candidate episodes, and zero modeled static-clearance-violation episodes.

P2–P1 mean seed effects were: outage −0.01740, mean SNR +0.2799 dB, minimum SNR +2.0097 dB, modeled BER −0.003045, and modeled goodput +3.045 Mbit/s. Every interval excluded zero and every Holm-adjusted p-value was below 0.001. These are modeled quantities.

P3 was worse than P2 across all five endpoints after Holm correction. This negative result rules out a claim that the evaluated uncertainty penalty improved communication utility. P4–P2 differences were not significant after correction; the evaluated practical predictor captured most of the oracle's modeled communication value under these conditions.

## 9. Mechanism ablation

The authorized 20-seed geometry study compared the directional model with a distance-only variant while holding planner and seeds fixed. The directional P2–P1 outage effect was −0.01803; the distance-only effect was approximately zero. The interaction supports directional geometry as a mechanism in this simulator. One directional/P2 episode had one no-candidate step, so the mechanism study is not an extension of the confirmatory safety claim.

## 10. Failure cases and negative results

Earlier protocol versions failed their declared safety gates. V6 failed confirmation because one seed caused no-candidate steps for P0, P1, and P3. V7 P3 underperformed P2. The geometry study contains one no-candidate episode. None is relabeled as a positive result.

## 11. Limitations and threats to validity

The link is analytical and uncalibrated against optical measurements. Target and ego motion are simulated. Safety gates demonstrate absence of specified failures only in sampled modeled scenarios, not safety guarantees. The endpoint-anchoring change followed diagnosis of a prior-version failure; scientific protection comes from freezing it before fresh V7 development and confirmatory seeds. P3 depends on the chosen uncertainty and risk model. The carrier/headlamp interpretation remains unresolved.

## 12. Discussion

The evidence supports the value of future trajectory-conditioned connectivity relative to reactive scoring under the evaluated model. It does not establish physical PC-FMCW performance. The nonsignificant oracle gap and negative P3 result suggest that added information or risk complexity is not automatically useful once a practical forecast is available.

## 13. Conclusion

Under the frozen controlled simulation, P2 improved modeled communication outcomes relative to P1 while all planners shared the same safety interface. This is algorithmic/model-based evidence only.
