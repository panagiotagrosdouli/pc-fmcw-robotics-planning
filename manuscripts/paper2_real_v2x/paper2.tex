\title{From QoS Prediction to Measured Decision Validity:\\
Support-Bounded Matched Field Replay for Vehicular Speed Decisions}

\begin{abstract}
Predictive quality-of-service (QoS) and communication-aware vehicle planning are established research directions, but logged field measurements create an evidential mismatch: models can score alternative motion actions even when the dataset contains no measured outcome for the action not taken. We study this mismatch using repeated CICV5G field drives and support-bounded matched field replay. Whole acquisition runs are assigned disjoint roles for predictor training, measured outcome donation, and decision queries; deployable rankings never observe donor outcomes before action selection. Under a frozen 10\% intervention budget, the primary W2S 30/50-km/h confirmation shows measured oracle headroom of $-4.177$ ms relative to FAST, with two-way query/donor bootstrap 95\% interval $[-13.659,-2.137]$ ms. The absolute-QoS policy retains $+2.704$ ms regret relative to that upper-bound diagnostic, while its comparison with FAST has interval $[-8.167,+0.657]$ ms and therefore does not establish superiority. A separately pre-frozen arterial n8 50/80-km/h confirmation again shows oracle headroom ($-0.590$ ms, $[-1.431,-0.250]$ ms), but the same predictive policy is worse than FAST ($+0.156$ ms, $[+0.038,+0.258]$ ms). A frozen pairwise action-margin model also fails to remove the gap. The result is an evidence-boundary finding: useful QoS prediction does not automatically constitute reliable motion-decision evidence when action support and acquisition-run dependence are enforced explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Connected and automated vehicles increasingly rely on wireless communication for cooperative perception, cloud-assisted services, infrastructure interaction, and distributed decision making. Vehicle motion changes the communication environment experienced by the platform, while anticipated communication quality can influence motion selection. This coupling is well established. Communication-aware motion planning has a long history \cite{ghaffarkhah2011,muralidharan2021}, including obstacle-aware formulations \cite{hurst2021}, joint motion--communication--sensing optimization \cite{cai2022}, QoS-aware autonomous-vehicle trajectory selection \cite{ullah2025}, radio-map planning \cite{gordon2026}, and uncertainty-aware connectivity control \cite{kim2026}.

Predictive QoS (PQoS) is likewise established in vehicular communications. Field campaigns and public datasets support data-driven prediction of delay, throughput, and radio indicators \cite{sliwa2018,hernangomez2023,palaios2023,partani2025,zhang2026}. Recent work predicts future radio maps \cite{cheng2026} and task-aware radio world models explicitly reason about downstream decision relevance \cite{wang2026rmworld}. Consequently, neither predicting connectivity nor using connectivity in motion planning is an adequate novelty claim.

The methodological problem studied here appears when a field-trained communication model is used to justify a different motion action. A logged drive records communication outcomes under the motion that was actually executed. A planner, however, asks which action should be selected among alternatives. If evaluation fills the unobserved action with the same model that made the decision, prediction and ground truth become circular. A favorable planner result can then reflect model self-consistency rather than measured decision evidence.

A second issue is statistical. Vehicular samples are correlated in time and space. Multiple query locations can share the same acquisition run, and multiple matched outcomes can reuse the same donor run. Treating timestamps as independent observations exaggerates effective sample size and can turn a favorable point estimate into an overstated performance claim. Prior PQoS work already shows strong sensitivity to data-split methodology \cite{palaios2023}; decision evaluation adds donor-run dependence on top of query-run dependence.

This paper asks a narrower question: \emph{when can repeated field measurements support a claim about a communication-aware vehicle motion decision without promoting model-generated counterfactuals to measured truth?}

We study this question on the CICV5G field dataset \cite{zhang2026}. The primary motion action is a physically interpretable 30/50-km/h speed choice at locations where separate repeated drives provide measured support for both actions. Complete acquisition runs are assigned disjoint roles for model training, measured outcome donation, and decision queries. A second pre-frozen arterial-road confirmation applies the same evidence protocol to a different 50/80-km/h action pair.

The contributions are deliberately evidence-centered:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We operationalize \emph{support-bounded matched field replay}, in which motion actions are evaluated only where disjoint repeated acquisition runs provide measured outcomes for the relevant action and context.
\item We separate predictor training, measured action-outcome donation, and decision querying across complete acquisition runs.
\item We evaluate both a conventional absolute-QoS ranking and a frozen pairwise action-margin ranking, retaining the negative result that direct margin prediction does not automatically solve downstream decision reliability.
\item We use a fixed intervention budget rather than hiding travel-time and network-delay units inside an arbitrary scalar objective.
\item We perform a locked W2S confirmation with two-way query-run and donor-run bootstrap resampling, then repeat the same frozen evidence protocol in a different arterial-road scenario. Both settings show measured action-value headroom and predictor regret; the arterial confirmation additionally shows the frozen predictive intervention performing worse than FAST.
\end{enumerate}

The contribution is not a new generic planner, a new decision-focused-learning paradigm, or a causal speed-effect estimate. It is a measured field-evidence protocol and an empirical demonstration that prediction-level usefulness and decision-level evidential validity can diverge.

\section{Related Work and Non-Overlap}
Communication-aware motion planning already establishes that communication quality can influence robot and vehicle motion \cite{ghaffarkhah2011,muralidharan2021,hurst2021,cai2022}. Ullah et al. study QoS-aware vehicle trajectory optimization \cite{ullah2025}, Gordon et al. use radio maps in motion planning \cite{gordon2026}, and Kim et al. propagate radio-map uncertainty into control \cite{kim2026}. These works establish the motion--communication coupling.

Field-measured PQoS is also established. Sliwa et al. combine mobility prediction and learned connectivity maps for vehicular communication \cite{sliwa2018}. Public drive-test datasets support geographically resolved communication modeling \cite{hernangomez2023,zhang2026}. Palaios et al. demonstrate strong sensitivity of vehicular QoS prediction to split methodology \cite{palaios2023}, while Partani et al. exploit leading-vehicle and historical information for urban V2X prediction \cite{partani2025}.

Decision-relevant communication modeling is likewise not new. RadioMapMotion targets proactive spatio-temporal radio prediction \cite{cheng2026}, and RMWorld values radio information according to downstream task relevance and credibility \cite{wang2026rmworld}. The novelty claim here is therefore not regret, decision-focused learning, or policy evaluation in general.

The distinction is the \emph{evaluation substrate}. Simulation, analytical propagation, ray tracing, or learned world models can provide a complete outcome surface for candidate actions. A finite field log cannot. The present study asks what conclusions survive when the evaluator refuses to invent missing field outcomes and when acquisition runs, rather than timestamps, determine the evidence structure.

\section{Field Data and Evidence Roles}
\subsection{CICV5G measurements}
CICV5G contains real 5G V2N2V measurements with synchronized communication and vehicle context, including delay, SINR, RSRP, position, heading, velocity, cell identity, and timestamps \cite{zhang2026}. The repository study uses repeated W2S/S2W acquisition runs under n8/n78 configurations and multiple vehicle speeds. A separately frozen out-of-scenario confirmation uses arterial-road n8 runs at 50 and 80 km/h, excluding aggregate files to avoid duplicate samples.

The earlier predictor audit uses 38 complete acquisition runs comprising 43,045 synchronized samples. That audit establishes two background facts: one-step persistence is a strong causal baseline, while spatial/context information can add predictive value at longer planning horizons. These facts motivate future prediction but do not establish that a motion action selected from that prediction is better.

\subsection{Disjoint acquisition-run roles}
Complete acquisition runs are partitioned within network, direction, and speed strata as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R
=
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{tr}}
\dot\cup
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{don}}
\dot\cup
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{qry}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{tr}}$ fits the predictive models, $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{don}}$ provides measured action outcomes, and $\mathcal R_{\mathrm{qry}}$ provides decision locations and contexts. No acquisition run serves more than one role in a configuration.

The locked confirmations use 40\% training, 40\% donor, and the remaining runs as query roles, with split seed 20260930 fixed before outcome inspection.

\section{Support-Bounded Matched Field Replay}
For query state $x$ and action $a$, define the donor support set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D(x,a)=
\left\{
i\in\mathcal R_{\mathrm{don}}:
c_i=c_x,\ a_i=a,\ \|p_i-p_x\|_2\le \rho
\right\},
\end{equation}
where $c$ denotes the frozen communication/motion context and $\rho=2$ m is the locked spatial caliper.

A matched measured outcome is reported only when $\mathcal D(x,a)$ is nonempty:
\begin{equation}
\tilde y(x,a)
=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal D^\star(x,a)|}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal D^\star(x,a)}y_i,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal D^\star$ is the selected donor subset, preferring donor-run diversity before repeated samples from one run. Both actions must have donor support for a query location to enter comparative evaluation. Query positions are thinned at 5-m spacing.

The donor outcome is never exposed to a deployable ranking before action selection. This strengthens the evaluation relative to model-as-truth replay, but the matched outcome remains observational. Separate acquisition runs can differ in network load, blockage, traffic, handovers, or other latent conditions. We therefore do not interpret $\tilde y(x,a)$ as a randomized potential outcome or a causal speed effect.

\section{Frozen Predictive Rankings}
\subsection{Absolute-QoS predictor}
A context-conditioned spatial k-nearest-neighbor predictor is fitted only on training-role runs. For the primary W2S actions, predicted communication gain from slowing is
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{pred}}(x)
=
\hat y(x,50)-\hat y(x,30).
\end{equation}
Positive gain means the model expects lower delay at the slower action. The arterial confirmation uses the same frozen architecture with the corresponding 50/80-km/h action pair.

\subsection{Pairwise action-margin predictor}
A second model is trained directly on matched action margins constructed only from training-role runs:
\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{margin}}(x)
=
\widehat{\tilde y(x,a_{\mathrm{fast}})
-\tilde y(x,a_{\mathrm{slow}})}.
\end{equation}
The model is a context-conditioned spatial KNN with 25 neighbors and no post-audit tuning. It is retained as a negative comparator rather than promoted as the paper's novelty.

\section{Budgeted Decision Policies}
Combining travel-time seconds and network-delay milliseconds in a single scalar objective requires an application-specific conversion that is not available in the dataset. We therefore use a maximum intervention budget. In each query run, at most fraction
\begin{equation}
B=0.10
\end{equation}
of supported locations may select the slower action.

The policies are:

\noindent\textbf{FAST:} always select the faster speed action.

\noindent\textbf{PRED\_BUDGET:} rank locations by $g_{\mathrm{pred}}$ and spend budget only where predicted gain is positive.

\noindent\textbf{MARGIN\_BUDGET:} rank by $g_{\mathrm{margin}}$ using the frozen pairwise model.

\noindent\textbf{ORACLE\_BUDGET:} rank by matched measured gain $\tilde y(x,a_{\mathrm{fast}})-\tilde y(x,a_{\mathrm{slow}})$. This policy is nondeployable and serves only as a support-bounded upper-bound diagnostic. Donor measurements enter its ranking, so it is not interpreted as an unbiased deployable-policy performance estimate.

\section{Statistical Protocol}
\subsection{Development sensitivity}
Before the locked confirmation, the replay was evaluated over five whole-run split seeds and spatial calipers of 1, 2, and 5 m, yielding 15 dependent development configurations. The finite drive pool is reused, so these configurations are summarized descriptively rather than treated as independent experiments.

Across these configurations, ORACLE\_BUDGET is favorable relative to FAST in all 15 for mean matched delay. In contrast, the conventional predicted-gain policy is favorable in only 9/15 for mean delay and 8/15 for matched p95 delay. The frozen pairwise margin hypothesis also fails its predeclared promising-method criterion.

\subsection{Locked confirmations}
The primary W2S confirmation fixes the split seed, 30/50-km/h action pair, 2-m caliper, 5-m query thinning, 40/40 train/donor fractions, 10\% intervention budget, and frozen model architectures before outcome inspection.

The arterial confirmation is frozen separately before outcome inspection. It uses arterial-road n8 measurements, a 50/80-km/h action pair, the same split seed and evidence-role fractions, the same 2-m caliper and 5-m thinning, and the same frozen model architectures and intervention budget.

\subsection{Two-way acquisition-run bootstrap}
A query run contributes many correlated query locations, and one donor run can contribute to many matched outcomes. We therefore independently resample query and donor acquisition runs.

For each bootstrap replicate:
\begin{enumerate}
\item query acquisition runs are sampled with replacement;
\item donor acquisition runs are sampled independently with replacement;
\item each matched action outcome is reconstructed from the resampled donor contributions;
\item ORACLE\_BUDGET is recomputed inside the replicate;
\item PRED\_BUDGET and MARGIN\_BUDGET keep their frozen model-based rankings;
\item mean-delay effects are aggregated at the query-run level.
\end{enumerate}

Each locked confirmation uses 5,000 replicates and percentile 95\% intervals. This finite-run bootstrap addresses the dominant two-way cluster dependence but does not convert observational repeated drives into a randomized experiment.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Prediction-level diagnostics do not settle the decision question}
The archived predictor study confirms that one-step persistence is difficult to beat. Persistence obtains 5.576 ms delay MAE on the primary grouped split, compared with 10.862, 7.150, and 6.659 ms for spatial kNN, Random Forest, and Extra Trees, respectively. At longer tested horizons, calibration-gated spatial/context prediction improves over persistence across all five grouped split assignments at 20, 50, and 100 steps.

These results establish predictive structure, but they do not establish downstream motion superiority.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Prediction utility is not measured decision evidence}
The predictor is not useless. It contains predictive structure, and on the W2S split its point estimate is favorable relative to FAST. The stronger cross-scenario result is that this is insufficient for a planner-superiority claim. In the arterial confirmation, the same frozen prediction-to-action logic is measurably worse than FAST.

The evidence therefore supports three statements: measurable action-value opportunity exists, the tested predictors retain regret relative to that opportunity, and the sign of the prediction-derived intervention relative to FAST is context-dependent.

\subsection{Why donor identity matters}
Repeated cellular drives are not interchangeable timestamps. Different donor runs can differ because of network load, blockage, cell state, traffic, and other latent factors. Development sweeps show substantial variation across run-role assignments.

The two-way bootstrap makes this dependence explicit. It weakens an apparently clean planner-performance story but strengthens the scientific validity of the conclusion by distinguishing favorable point estimates from effects that survive acquisition-run uncertainty.

\subsection{Why the pairwise failure matters}
Decision-focused learning and task-relevant prediction are established research directions. Merely replacing absolute QoS loss with an action-margin target is therefore not a sufficient novelty claim. The frozen experiment provides a more useful negative result: direct pairwise margin prediction does not reliably recover the support-bounded opportunity and is measurably worse than FAST in the arterial setting.

This indicates that downstream reliability depends on more than the supervised target. Finite action overlap, donor quality, latent run context, and acquisition-run uncertainty remain part of the problem.

\subsection{Relationship to task-aware radio models}
Task-aware radio world models such as RMWorld reason explicitly about downstream decision relevance and counterfactual credibility \cite{wang2026rmworld}. The present study is complementary. We do not propose a richer world model; we audit what can be claimed when measured field logs are the evidence source and unsupported actions remain unevaluated rather than synthesized.

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
First, matched field replay is observational. The compared speeds come from separate repeated drives, not randomized paired interventions at identical times. Network load, traffic, blockage, handovers, and other conditions can differ. The reported effects are support-bounded matched outcomes, not causal speed effects.

Second, the measured oracle is an upper-bound diagnostic, not a deployable controller and not an unbiased estimate of future policy performance. It uses donor measurements for action ranking.

Third, the number of independent acquisition runs remains modest, especially in the arterial confirmation. The two-way bootstrap resamples the available query and donor clusters but cannot create new independent field experiments.

Fourth, spatial matching is intentionally simple. A 2-m caliper plus exact communication/action context cannot remove all latent context mismatch. Richer matching may reduce bias but can also reduce action overlap.

Fifth, the 10\% intervention budget is a methodological operating point rather than an application-specific optimal speed policy.

Finally, CICV5G validates only the measured 5G/V2N2V branch studied here. It does not validate the separate PC-FMCW optical communication model maintained elsewhere in the repository.

\section{Conclusion}
Communication-aware motion planning and predictive QoS are mature research areas. The unresolved issue studied here is evidential: when a field-trained connectivity model recommends a different vehicle motion, what measured evidence supports the resulting decision claim?

Using repeated CICV5G drives, we separate model training, measured action-outcome donation, and decision queries across complete acquisition runs. A locked W2S two-way query/donor bootstrap reveals clear support-bounded measured action-value headroom while the prediction-derived rankings retain regret and do not establish superiority over FAST. A separately pre-frozen arterial n8 confirmation reproduces the action-value headroom with a different 50/80-km/h pair and shows both frozen prediction-derived interventions performing measurably worse than FAST.

The conclusion is intentionally narrow: prediction accuracy, predictive utility, and measured motion-decision validity are different quantities. Field-measured communication models should not be promoted from regression evidence to planner-superiority claims unless action support, outcome provenance, and acquisition-run dependence are made explicit.
